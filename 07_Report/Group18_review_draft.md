## Model geometry and mesh

FDS 6.10.1 represents the 6.60 m long, 0.90 m wide and 0.76 m high compartment. The computational domain extends to y = 1.40 m outside the opening wall so that doorway inflow and outflow can develop. The ambient temperature is 27.5 °C. The nominal propane burner is a 0.25 m square. At 106.2 kW its prescribed heat release per unit area is 1,699.2 kW/m². The ceiling comparison uses 1 mm thermocouples at z = 0.74 m. For the OP-1 mesh study the coarse, medium and fine domains contain 132 × 28 × 19, 176 × 36 × 25 and 220 × 48 × 32 cells respectively. All three inputs specify the same burner position and dimensions. The FDS mesh-face indices give burner footprints of 5 × 5, 6 × 7 and 8 × 9 cells on the coarse, medium and fine grids, respectively. Multiplying by the corresponding horizontal cell areas gives 0.06250, 0.06125 and 0.06300 m², compared with the nominal 0.06250 m². ## Propylene smoke extraction

Raising both end inlet tops from 0.36 to 0.44 m increased their combined area from 0.648 to 0.792 m². At soot yield 0.05 kg/kg, the modified layout gave a thermal layer height of 0.760 m at both reference breach locations: x = 0.5 m at 17 s in OP-1 (reference 0.240 m), and x = 6.1 m at 83 s in OP-6 (reference 0.353 m). Each branch delivered 0.640 m³/s and half-height visibility was 30 m in all four records. These paired samples test whether the original local excursions recur. The design brief specifies propylene with a baseline soot yield of 0.02 kg/kg and a CO yield of 0.01 kg/kg, followed by soot yields of 0.05 and 0.10 kg/kg for the more sooty scenarios. The project evaluates OP-1 and OP-6 at a nominal design fire of 106.2 kW, the higher of the two Group 18 validation powers. Screening at soot yield 0.05 can eliminate a candidate early. Recommending a surviving design requires separate evidence for the baseline and the other agreed scenarios. [Design and optimisation requirements](../01_Assignment_Brief/Project_Topic_1B_Smoke_Extraction.pdf)

A steady sensible-heat balance gives an initial scale for extraction. The hot-gas volume flow is Qᵥ = H/(ρcₚΔT), where H is the sensible heat carried out in kW. Assuming H = 0.65 × 106.2 kW, cₚ = 1.0 kJ/(kg·K) and ideal-gas density at the exhaust temperature, rises of 50, 100 and 200 K above 300.65 K give 1.37, 0.78 and 0.49 m³/s. Both the heat fraction and outlet temperature rises are assumptions. Ceiling impingement, bidirectional door flow and entrainment in the 0.76 m compartment limit the uniform-layer approximation. CFD evaluates the layer height for each layout and flow. [Heat-balance assumptions and calculation](../08_Project_Notes/05_Group18_当前执行与诊断.md) · [NISTIR 5516](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir5516.pdf)

The brief requires the smoke layer to stay above or at half the 0.76 m compartment height, giving a minimum of 0.38 m above the floor. For this project, the numerical screening rule uses eight centreline stations over a 120 s fire. One valid recorded thermal layer height below 0.38 m eliminates a candidate. A passing screen covers the eight sampled locations and recorded times. The temperature and extinction slices resolve the longitudinal plane at y = 0.45 m and can reveal smoke between stations. For the two completed 0.05 kg/kg cases, the three-dimensional soot output was also checked along y = 0.15, 0.45 and 0.75 m. At each approximately 1 s output, adjacent x nodes counted towards a continuous span only if their encoded soot values were positive at both z = 0.36 and 0.40 m. The longest spans across those lines occurred at y = 0.75 m: 1.10 m in OP-1 at 3.00 s and 0.95 m in OP-6 at 4.00 s. These near-fire spans describe detectable soot around half height. Their interpretation includes the plume and depends on the encoding resolution. ## Search and fan power

The replay fixes the opening configuration at OP-6 and the soot yield at 0.02 kg/kg. Its 16 candidates combine four curtain depths (0, 0.08, 0.16 and 0.24 m) with four total flows (0.08, 0.16, 0.24 and 0.32 m³/s). Each margin uses the lowest height across eight stations over 0–120 s. A negative margin in this single scenario suffices to eliminate a design required to pass every scenario. Ranking door configurations or soot yields requires matched runs of the same design across scenarios. All three searches start from the same four corner designs. Each query reveals one archived FDS margin. Ordered search then increases flow, breaking ties by curtain depth. BO weights the predicted probability of feasibility by potential flow reduction. The GA crosses and mutates pairs of curtain depth and flow, ranking candidates with the surrogate mean. Query counts measure how early each method found the best observed margin on this grid. The replay provides no estimate of the number of CFD runs needed to discover a feasible design outside it. [Replay implementation](../06_Tools/replay_search.py) · [Query histories](../05_Calculations/search_replay.csv)

The first design objective is to find a nonnegative minimum layer-height margin in every agreed scenario. Only then can fan power be minimized among feasible layouts. In the offline replay, the largest margin among the original 16 candidates was −0.285 m at 0.16 m curtain depth and 0.32 m³/s extraction. Bayesian optimization reached this candidate on query 7 and ordered search on query 16. Across 20 random seeds the surrogate GA reached it in 6–11 queries (median 9). All three methods exhausted the same design set without finding a feasible candidate. For each 0.40 × 0.80 m branch, the assumed pressure loss is Δp = (fL/D + K)ρv²/2, with hydraulic diameter D = 0.533 m, length L = 0.40 m, Darcy friction factor f = 0.02, combined local loss coefficient K = 2 and air density ρ = 1.2 kg/m³. The branch speed v uses half the total flow divided by the branch area. For the wider exhaust openings, the two short branches together require an estimated 10.32 W at 1.28 m³/s under the assumed branch loss of 4.84 Pa and total efficiency of 0.60. At 0.96 m³/s, the same two branches each carry 0.48 m³/s at 1.50 m/s. The assumed branch loss falls to 2.72 Pa, giving a combined input of 0.96 × 2.72 / 0.60 = 4.35 W. For the two completed enlarged-inlet cases at 0.05 kg/kg soot yield, applying the same loss model to each recorded branch flow and integrating the summed power by the trapezoidal rule gives 1.223 kJ over 120 s (10.19 W mean) for each door configuration. FDS prescribes branch flow in these cases. Pressure loss and efficiency are engineering assumptions in both power estimates. Final fan selection requires the full system pressure rise and a design that meets the height criterion. ## Supplementary surrogate check

The physics-informed surrogate was trained on FDS output to predict eight station height histories. A held-out design tested extrapolation from curtain depths of 0.16 and 0.24 m to 0.32 m at OP-6, soot yield 0.10 and total flow 1.28 m³/s. Its predicted minimum of 0.619 m exceeded the full-run FDS minimum of 0.267 m by 0.352 m and would have classified the design as passing. The independent reference here is a held-out CFD case; experimental validation is a separate propane comparison. This false pass is why the design decision uses full-duration FDS output or an observed early failure. [Surrogate methodology and event audit](../09_PINN_Layer/README.md)

## Effect of increasing end-opening height

Increasing both end openings from 0.44 to 0.48 m at the same exhaust flow of 1.28 m³/s allowed the OP-6 case with soot yield 0.10 kg/kg to complete 120 s above the height criterion at all eight stations. The lowest layer height (0.550 m) and lowest half-height visibility (3.22 m) occurred together at x = 3.6 m and t = 4.00 s. The highest half-height CO concentration was 37.9 ppm at x = 3.0 m and t = 76.00 s. The layer criterion therefore leaves distinct local visibility and CO conditions to report. [Eight-station results](../05_Calculations/inlet48_6_0p1_0p24_1p28_120_stations.csv)

At 4.004 s, the longest connected run of detectable soot on the three sampled longitudinal lines extended from x = 3.0 to 3.9 m on the centreline. At x = 3.25 m on that line, the nodes bracketing half height (z = 0.36 and 0.40 m) both contained detectable soot at every recorded frame from 1.003 to 120 s. The eight-station height result therefore coexisted with persistent soot near the burner. These three lines alone cannot locate a compartment-wide smoke interface.

\reportheading{5.\quad Surrogate assessment for short smoke-layer excursions}
A surrogate could reduce the number of CFD evaluations if it identifies the designs closest to violating the height criterion. We tested this proposition using complete held-out designs. The dataset comprised 16 original-layout and six raised-inlet OP-6 cases. Each test withheld all times and all stations of one design. For the eight-output physics-informed model, the initial height was fixed at 0.76~m and a simplified upper-layer volume balance constrained the spatially averaged response. We scored both the lowest predicted height and the overlap of individual predicted and recorded threshold excursions.

A linear scalar model predicted original-layout minima with a mean absolute error of 0.96~cm. On the raised-inlet cases that error increased to 6.13~cm. The time-series PINN missed all 11 short events in the six raised-inlet designs and classified all six as passing. The records in Figure 4 show how smoothing a brief drop at a single station can alter a design decision.

Eight held-out layer-height series, OP-6, 0.24~m curtains and soot yield 0.05. At $x=6.1$~m and 83.0~s the recorded FDS value was 0.353~m and the PINN prediction was 0.750~m.}

An independent extrapolation used 0.32~m curtains and soot yield 0.10. FDS recorded a minimum of 0.267~m, while the PINN predicted 0.619~m. Increasing the training weight on samples near and below the threshold still yielded zero matches among 178 short events across the two layouts. The spatially averaged balance offers limited information on local minima. Within these data, full CFD time histories remain necessary to judge candidates close to the design threshold.

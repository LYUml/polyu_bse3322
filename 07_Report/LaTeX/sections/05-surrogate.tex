\reportheading{5.\quad Pre-screening accuracy and missed excursions}
\subhead{Predicting the design minimum before an FDS run}
For the pre-screen, 27 complete FDS runs covered 26 physical designs across OP-1 and OP-6. Each test withheld one whole design, including all its mesh variants. Mean absolute errors in the minimum layer height were 6.97~cm for the corrected PINN, 7.39~cm for direct regression and 6.70~cm for their equal-weight average. Of eight runs with a breach, these estimators missed seven, four and four, respectively. Among nineteen runs meeting the criterion, they incorrectly flagged zero, one and zero. In a separate test withholding entire inlet-layout families, all three estimators missed all 14 observed breaches (Table~F2).

Predictions frozen before the last three 1.12~m$^3$/s runs illustrate the decision risk. The hybrid underestimated the OP-1 minima by 1.7 and 5.2~cm at soot yields 0.05 and 0.10. For the limiting OP-6 case at yield 0.05, the PINN alone predicted 0.5766~m and the hybrid predicted 0.5576~m. FDS gave 0.4358~m. The PINN overestimated clearance by 140.8~mm and the hybrid by 121.8~mm. Both predicted a pass, but the verified margin was only 55.8~mm. These forecasts can guide run order. The six FDS histories establish feasibility.

\subhead{Why promising designs still require FDS}
An earlier time-series test held out complete designs from 16 original-layout and six raised-inlet OP-6 cases. The eight-output PINN imposed an initial height of 0.76~m and a simplified upper-layer volume balance. A linear scalar model estimated minimum height with mean absolute errors of 0.96~cm on the original layout and 6.13~cm on the raised-inlet cases. The time-series PINN classified all six raised-inlet designs as passing and missed all 11 short events. Figure~5 shows one such excursion.

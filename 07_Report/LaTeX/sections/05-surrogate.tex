\subhead{5.1\quad Minimum-height predictions and FDS verification}
A brief fall below 0.38~m at any of the eight stations is enough to breach the 120~s design criterion. We therefore compared forecasts using the lowest FDS reading in each run (time-series errors are in Appendix~E). In the first 20 runs on the 19-cell vertical grid, correcting each station minimum reduced the mean absolute error (MAE) in the compartment minimum from 27.03 to 8.26~cm. The larger sample included 27 completed runs for 26 designs across OP-1 and OP-6. Each design and its mesh variants were held out together. MAE was 6.97~cm for the corrected PINN, 7.39~cm for direct regression and 6.70~cm for their equal-weight average. Of the eight actual breaches the models missed seven, four and four, respectively. Among the 19 passing runs, the same models raised zero, one and zero false alarms. Table~F2 extends the comparison to whole inlet-layout families.

The open diamonds in Figure~5 show predictions made before the FDS results were available. For OP-1 at soot yields 0.05 and 0.10, the PINN underestimated minimum height by 5.1 and 34.8~mm. The hybrid underestimated it by 1.7 and 5.2~cm. The error changed direction in the case governing the selected 1.12~m$^3$/s flow. For OP-6 at yield 0.05, the PINN gave 0.5766~m and the hybrid 0.5576~m, overpredicting the FDS minimum by 140.8 and 121.8~mm. From the six refined-grid runs the lowest recorded height was 0.4358~m, giving the design margin of 55.8~mm (Table~D3).

\subhead{5.2\quad What the two search-order replays measured}
In the original layout, one query returned the FDS margin for one of 16 designs. The fixed order reached the design with the largest recorded margin on its 16th query. BO reached it on query 7 and GA on queries 6--11 across 20 random seeds.

For the revised layout the comparison starts with the lowest-power flow and asks which of its six scenarios to check first. The fixed order starts with OP-6, then checks OP-1, taking the soot yields in ascending order for each. Its first query exposed the breach at 0.96~m$^3$/s and its next six confirmed 1.12~m$^3$/s. Checking the lowest predicted height first took 7--11 queries with the PINN, seven with direct regression and 7--9 with their equal-weight average. The ranges allow a missing archived result to be either a pass or a breach (Figure~C1).

We also allowed the forecasts to change the flow order. Flows predicted to pass were checked first, in increasing power order, starting with the lowest predicted clearance at each flow. We counted queries first to find a flow that passed all six scenarios and then to rule out all lower-power tested flows. On the original grid every method met both conditions in six checks. On the refined grid the fixed order took seven for both. The PINN took 7--17 to find a passing flow and 9--17 to establish the lowest-power feasible choice. Direct regression took seven for both, and the hybrid 7--9 for both. The ranges cover every pass or breach assignment for the missing runs.

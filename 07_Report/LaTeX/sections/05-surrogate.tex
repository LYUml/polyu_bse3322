\reportheading{5.\quad Pre-screening accuracy and missed excursions}
\subhead{Predicting the design minimum before an FDS run}
Across 22 complete FDS runs, we held out each of 21 physical designs in turn, keeping its mesh variants together. Mean absolute errors in the minimum layer height were 7.34~cm for the corrected PINN, 7.85~cm for direct regression and 6.99~cm for their equal-weight average. Of eight runs with a breach, these estimators missed seven, four and four, respectively. Among fourteen runs meeting the criterion, they incorrectly flagged zero, one and one. Averaging improved the minimum-height estimate slightly while retaining the regression model's error counts at the threshold. Savings in new FDS runs remain unmeasured.

\subhead{Why promising designs still require FDS}
An earlier time-series test held out complete designs from 16 original-layout and six raised-inlet OP-6 cases. The eight-output PINN imposed an initial height of 0.76~m and a simplified upper-layer volume balance. A linear scalar model estimated minimum height with mean absolute errors of 0.96~cm on the original layout and 6.13~cm on the raised-inlet cases. The time-series PINN classified all six raised-inlet designs as passing and missed all 11 short events. Figure~5 shows one such excursion.

For a separate 0.32~m curtain trial at soot yield 0.10, FDS recorded a minimum of 0.267~m and the PINN predicted 0.619~m. Emphasising near-threshold samples in training still yielded zero matches in 178 short events across the original and raised-inlet layouts.

Reconstructing 19 temperatures per column and applying the FDS layer-height integrals also missed local events. For the 0.32~m curtain it flagged a breach, but neither alarm matched any of the 13 short excursions. On the 16 original designs, averaging the reconstruction with the PINN reduced mean curve error from 4.55 to 4.26~cm while missing all 167 short events. Such errors matter when one brief drop at a single station can reject a design.
